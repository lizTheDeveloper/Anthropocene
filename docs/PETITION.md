## II. Statement of Grounds

### A. Soil Erosion: The Physical Foundation Is Being Lost

US cropland currently erodes at approximately 25 times the rate of soil formation (Thaler et al. 2022; Evan et al. 2021). In the Corn Belt, 35% of cultivated land has lost its entire A-horizon — the organic-matter-enriched topsoil layer that sustains fertility — representing 57.6 billion tons of topsoil lost over 160 years of cultivation (Evan et al. 2021, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 118(8): e1922375118).

The 2017 Natural Resources Inventory (NRI) reports national cropland erosion at 4.63 tons per acre per year, with total annual soil loss of 1.70 billion tons. While erosion rates declined 31.8% between 1982 and 2017, 89% of that reduction was achieved before 1997. Since 2007, water erosion has increased 2.7%. As of the most recent NRI data, 56.5 million acres (18% of US cropland) erode above the tolerable soil loss rate (T), the threshold NRCS defines as sustainable.

Globally, agriculture has depleted an estimated 133 gigatons of soil carbon since cultivation began (Sanderman et al. 2017, *PNAS* 114(36): 9575–9580). In the United States, the sole national soil carbon measurement — the USDA Rapid Carbon Assessment (RaCA) — is a single cross-sectional snapshot taken circa 2010, comprising approximately 1,263 lab-measured cropland pedons. There are zero repeat measurements. The USDA Soil Carbon Monitoring Network (SCMN) will not produce its first repeat data until FY2030 at the earliest.

The word "carbon" does not appear in the 222-page 2022 NRI Summary Report.

### B. Pesticide Composition Shift: Substitution, Not Reduction

Analysis of USGS Pesticide National Synthesis Project (PNSP) county-level estimates for 1992–2018, corrected for the exclusion of California in 2017–2018 data and the removal of double-counted aggregate rows, shows:

- **Total applied mass is roughly flat**, increasing 14.7% over 26 years (+2.3% on a fixed compound roster).
- **Glyphosate mass increased 17-fold**, from 6.9 million kg (1.8% of total) to 119.2 million kg (26.6% of total).
- **Chemical diversity collapsed.** The effective number of compounds in the national mixture (Shannon entropy, exponentiated) fell from 23.8 to 10.4. At the county level, the effective toolkit shrank from 9 to 4.8 compounds.
- **This is substitution, not reduction or redesign.** One chemical replaced many, concentrating ecological risk in a single molecule.

Cover crops, the sole indicator of structural agroecological redesign in the federal record, reached 4.7% of US cropland in the 2022 Census of Agriculture. At the realized adoption rate, cover crops would require approximately 225 years to reach 50% of US cropland.

**Note on data integrity:** The PNSP data contains a structural break at 2014/2015. Beginning in 2015, seed treatment applications were excluded from the underlying survey data. This caused neonicotinoid EPest-high mass to fall 85.3% in a single step (clothianidin: −99.4%; thiamethoxam: −80.7%; imidacloprid: −66.1%), and total insecticide mass to drop approximately 20%. Any insecticide trend crossing 2014/2015 reflects a survey methodology change, not a field practice change. This has been verified against the metadata files accompanying all five preliminary PNSP releases.

### C. The Soil-Biology Evidence Gap: 98.8% of Applied Mass Is Untested

Cross-referencing EPA's ECOTOX Knowledgebase (full ASCII release) against PNSP applied-mass data reveals:

- **98.8%** of applied pesticide mass has zero records on soil bacteria in ECOTOX.
- **96.3%** has no field evidence on earthworms.
- **97.5%** has no records on mycorrhizal fungi.
- Only **0.87%** of applied mass reaches the threshold of multi-year field evidence supported by two or more studies.
- **79%** of applied mass has no comparable acute soil-fauna toxicity endpoint of any kind.
- Glyphosate — the single most-applied compound — has 106 soil-fauna records in ECOTOX but **zero comparable endpoints** and **zero soil-bacterial records**.

This gap exists because 40 CFR § 158.630, which specifies the data requirements for pesticide registration, **contains no soil organism testing requirement**. This is a regulatory design choice, not an absence of scientific concern. The European Union requires soil organism data under Regulation (EU) No. 283/2013.

The measurement gap extends beyond toxicology. In a systematic assessment of seven agroecosystem service outcomes — yield, profitability, erosion, carbon storage, nutrient cycling, pest regulation, and water capture — four (nutrient cycling, pest regulation, water capture, soil macrofauna) have zero federal observation cells in 41 years of records. The maximum number of these outcomes simultaneously observable in the same place and year is three, at state resolution, in five years.

Hay — the most widely grown crop in the United States by farm count (612,946 farms, 32.3% of all operations) — has never been surveyed for pesticide use under NASS's Agricultural Chemical Use program.

### D. Nutritional Decline in Crops

Comparison of USDA food composition data for 43 garden crops between 1950 and 1999 revealed statistically reliable declines in six nutrients: protein (−6%), calcium, phosphorus, iron, riboflavin (−38%), and ascorbic acid (Davis, Epp & Riordan 2004, *Journal of the American College of Nutrition* 23(6): 669–682).

Three independent mechanisms contribute to this decline:

1. **The dilution effect:** Breeding for higher yield dilutes mineral concentration per unit mass. This is well-established and accounts for a significant portion of observed decline (Davis 2009).
2. **Rising atmospheric CO₂:** Free-Air CO₂ Enrichment (FACE) experiments demonstrate that elevated CO₂ directly reduces protein, zinc, and iron in C3 crops. A 2025 meta-analysis of 29,524 observation pairs confirmed this effect across multiple crop species (ter Haar et al. 2025).
3. **Soil biological disruption:** Disruption of mycorrhizal fungal networks and soil microbial communities reduces mineral uptake efficiency. The Broadbalk wheat experiment (Rothamsted, continuous since 1843) showed soil mineral concentrations remaining stable while grain mineral concentrations declined — indicating the minerals are present in soil but plants are unable to access them with the same efficiency (Fan et al. 2008, *Journal of Trace Elements in Medicine and Biology* 22(4): 315–324).

Montgomery and Biklé's 2022 paired-farm comparison found that regenerative farms (no-till, cover crops, diverse rotations, 5–10 years of practice) produced measurably more nutritious crops on the same land: wheat showed +41% boron, +48% calcium, +56% zinc, +29% magnesium (Montgomery & Biklé 2022, *PeerJ* 10: e12848).

### E. Human Health Evidence

#### Children's body burden

CDC NHANES data spanning 2013–2023 (n ≈ 31,000) shows that children aged 3–11 carry approximately 1.9 times the adult body burden for glyphosate, TCPy (a chlorpyrifos metabolite), and 2,4-D. Creatinine-adjusted concentrations widen this gap. Pesticide detection rates are universal and income-flat (77–99% detection, <3 percentage points between income quintiles), while nutritional deficiency rates are sharply income-stratified (2–2.5× gap between poorest and richest quintiles for iron, vitamin D, and folate).

#### Neurodevelopmental evidence

Three independent prospective birth cohort studies have found associations between prenatal pesticide exposure and adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes:

- **CHAMACOS** (Center for the Health Assessment of Mothers and Children of Salinas, UC Berkeley, PI: Eskenazi): Organophosphate metabolites in maternal urine associated with lower IQ and adverse neurobehavioral outcomes at age 7.
- **Columbia Center for Children's Environmental Health** (PI: Rauh): Prenatal chlorpyrifos exposure associated with structural brain changes visible on MRI, including thinning of cortical regions involved in attention and cognition.
- **CHARGE Study** (Childhood Autism Risks from Genetics and the Environment, UC Davis, PI: Hertz-Picciotto): Residential proximity to organophosphate application during pregnancy associated with increased risk of autism spectrum disorder.

#### Cancer

The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classified glyphosate as "probably carcinogenic to humans" (Group 2A) in 2015. A 2026 meta-analysis found a statistically significant association between glyphosate exposure and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (RR 1.29). The Ramazzini Institute's 2025 chronic bioassay reported tumor formation in animals exposed at the European Union's accepted daily intake — the dose regulators had classified as "safe."

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit vacated EPA's determination that glyphosate is "not likely to be carcinogenic to humans," finding that EPA failed to follow its own cancer assessment guidelines.

#### Gut microbiome

Glyphosate acts via the shikimate pathway, which is absent in mammals but present in bacteria. Peer-reviewed metagenomics studies (2023) confirmed that glyphosate selectively inhibits beneficial gut bacteria (Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium) while sparing pathogenic species — a differential sensitivity that the shikimate pathway mechanism predicts.

#### The US–EU regulatory gap

Seventy-two (72) pesticide active ingredients currently registered for use in the United States are banned in the European Union. US agriculture applies approximately 322 million pounds of EU-banned pesticides annually. The EU applies the precautionary principle under Regulation (EC) No. 1107/2009; the United States requires proof of unreasonable adverse effects under FIFRA § 2(bb). This regulatory divergence constitutes a natural experiment: two comparable economies, two regulatory philosophies, divergent exposure profiles.

## III. Responses to Anticipated Counterarguments

This petition acknowledges and addresses the strongest objections to the requested actions:

### A. The dilution effect explains crop nutrient decline, not soil depletion.

**Acknowledged.** The dilution effect (Davis 2009) — cultivar selection for yield reducing mineral concentration per unit mass — is a real and well-established phenomenon that accounts for a significant portion of observed nutrient decline. This petition does not rest on the claim that soil mineral depletion is the sole or even primary cause of nutritional decline.

The petition's nutritional argument rests on three points that the dilution effect does not address: (1) that soil biological disruption by pesticides reduces mineral uptake efficiency independent of cultivar (supported by Broadbalk data showing stable soil minerals with declining grain minerals); (2) that regenerative practices demonstrably increase crop nutrient density on the same land (Montgomery & Biklé 2022); and (3) that the federal measurement system lacks the capacity to distinguish these causes at national scale.

### B. Organic/regenerative yields are lower, threatening food security.

**Acknowledged.** Meta-analyses report organic yield gaps of 19–25% on average (Seufert et al. 2012, *Nature*; Ponisio et al. 2015). This petition does not request an overnight ban on conventional agriculture or mandatory organic certification.

The requested actions are phased (3–5 year timelines), targeted at specific high-risk compounds rather than all synthetic inputs, and paired with transition funding. The yield gap narrows with management skill and time (Ponisio et al. found management practices matter more than crop type). LaCanne and Lundgren (2018, *PeerJ*) documented 78% higher profits on regenerative corn fields despite 29% lower yields, because input costs fell from 32% to 12% of gross income.

### C. Sri Lanka's overnight agrochemical ban caused catastrophic food production loss.

**Acknowledged.** Sri Lanka's April 2021 ban on all agrochemical imports, implemented overnight with no farmer training program, no organic input supply chain, and no transition period, caused rice production to fall 20% in six months and overall food production to decline 40–50%. The ban was revoked after seven months.

This outcome validates the necessity of the transition infrastructure this petition requests. It does not validate the status quo. Every action requested here includes transition timelines, farmer support mechanisms, and phased implementation — precisely the elements Sri Lanka omitted.

### D. >99% of food residues are within EPA tolerance levels.

**Acknowledged.** The 2024 Pesticide Data Program found 99% of 9,872 samples had residues below EPA tolerances. However, tolerances are established on the basis of acute toxicity assessments and do not incorporate chronic low-dose endocrine disruption, mixture effects (27 studies document synergistic effects of pesticide combinations), developmental neurotoxicity at sub-acute levels, or effects on the gut microbiome. This petition requests that the adequacy of the tolerance-setting framework itself be evaluated, not merely that existing tolerances be enforced.

### E. Soil carbon sequestration cannot offset emissions at the scale claimed.

**Acknowledged.** Powlson et al. (Rothamsted) have demonstrated significant limitations to the "4 per 1000" aspiration in temperate regions. This petition does not claim soil carbon sequestration as a standalone climate solution. It requests soil health standards that include carbon as one of several indicators (alongside aggregate stability, biological activity, and erosion rate), in service of agricultural sustainability, not as a substitute for emissions reduction.

## VI. Economic Impact Statement

The petitioner acknowledges that the requested actions will impose transition costs on agricultural operations. Based on available evidence:

- **Yield gap during transition:** 19–25% average, narrowing to near-parity within 3–5 years with best management practices (Ponisio et al. 2015; Rodale Institute 40-year data).
- **Long-term profitability:** Regenerative operations documented at 78% higher profitability due to input cost reduction from 32% to 12% of gross income (LaCanne & Lundgren 2018).
- **Current economic loss from erosion:** A-horizon loss in the Corn Belt causes an average 6% crop yield reduction, representing billions in annual foregone production (Evan et al. 2021).
- **Current annual soil loss:** 1.70 billion tons, with estimated economic loss of $12.75 billion per year.

The requested Regenerative Transition Fund is designed to cover the transition-period yield gap, ensuring that the cost of the transition is borne by the public (which benefits from soil conservation, water quality, and public health outcomes), not solely by individual farm operations.
