## Why TDS is the priority

TDS is the only federal program that measures pesticide residues **and**
micronutrient content **in the same food samples**, prepared as consumed, over
time, at detection limits 10–100× lower than the regulatory program. Every other
route to "residues up, nutrients down" forces a join across incompatible
sampling frames. In TDS that comparison is internally valid.

TDS pesticide results through FY2017 are inside the pesticide monitoring
reports; FY2018 onward are posted only on the TDS site. Both locations needed.

## Caveat to pre-empt

PDP's own headline is that >99% of samples fall below EPA benchmark levels. The
argument cannot be "residues exceed tolerances." It has to be about cumulative
and aggregate exposure, the adequacy of tolerance-setting, or soil and ecosystem
effects that residue testing does not measure at all.

## Corrections to the source brief

- PDP archive begins at **1992**, not 1991.
- The commodity set rotates annually in coordination with EPA, so year-over-year
  detection rates are not comparable without conditioning on commodity mix.
