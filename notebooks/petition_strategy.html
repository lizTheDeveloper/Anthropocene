Petition strategyEvidence assessment14 Sep 2026

Which petition survives the evidence

The original thesis was that nutritional, soil and toxicological data together show American soil being strip-mined — and that this justifies banning pesticides and mandating regenerative practice. Tested against the federal record, the central claim does not hold. Three narrower asks do, and one of them is strong.

Verdict on the original framing

Cropland eroding faster than soil forms fell from 25.4% to 18.0% since 1982. Applied pesticide mass is flat. Iron status in American women has not worsened in sixteen years. A petition built on "it is getting worse" would be rebutted using the agencies' own data, in their own comment period.

Step 01 — The evidence ledger

Every claim in the thesis, and what the data says

Each row is a proposition the original argument needs. The status column is what this project's analysis found, not what would be convenient.

Proposition
Status
What the record shows
US soil is being strip-mined — erosion is accelerating
Refuted
NRI 1982–2022: cropland eroding above T fell 25.4%→18.0% (water) and 21.4%→14.6% (wind). 41–43% fewer acres. Soil is eroding less.
Americans apply ever more pesticide
Refuted
PNSP 1992–2018: +2.3% on a fixed compound roster. The apparent recent 10% fall was a California artifact; the rise is not there either.
Falling food nutrient density shows up in human nutritional status
Refuted
NHANES: iron deficiency in women 12–49 flat at 14.0%→14.6% over sixteen years; median ferritin rose 19%.
Soil depletion — not cultivar change — explains micronutrient decline
Untested
The dilution effect (selection for yield) is the standard explanation. Defeating it needs same-cultivar or archived-sample comparison. Not attempted here.
A meaningful share of cropland is losing soil faster than it forms
Supported
56.5M acres — one cultivated acre in six — erode above T in 2022. Non-regenerative at that level, just not accelerating.
Progress on soil loss has stalled
Supported
Water erosion bottomed at 17.4% in 2007 and has risen 0.7 points since. Wind bottomed 2012. Four decades of gains stopped.
Children carry disproportionate pesticide exposure
Supported
NHANES: children 3–11 at 1.83–1.88× adult burden for glyphosate, TCPy and 2,4-D. Creatinine correction widened every gap.
EPA lacks soil-ecotoxicity data on what it licenses
Supported
ECOTOX: 16 of 21 major compounds have zero comparable acute soil-fauna endpoints, covering 79% of applied mass. Glyphosate: 106 records, none comparable.
Pesticide exposure is concentrated among the poor
Refuted
Detection is near-universal and flat across income (glyphosate 77.6%, TCPy 98.8%; spread under 3 points). Nutritional deficiency, by contrast, is steeply income-sorted.
Step 02 — What actually moved the soil

The lever is land retirement, not chemistry

Erosion improved sharply from 1982, then stopped improving around 2007. The variable that tracks that shape is not pesticide volume — which barely moved — but the acreage retired from production under the Conservation Reserve Program.

CRP went from zero in 1982 to 33.3 million acres by 1992, held near that level through 2007, then collapsed 70% to 10.2 million by 2022. The erosion stall dates from the same period.

This is an association across two series, not a controlled comparison, and it should be presented as one. But it is a mechanism a regulator can act on, with a budget line attached — which "ban a compound" is not, because compound volume did not drive the trend in the first place.

Step 03 — The asks

Three petitions the evidence will carry

Ranked by how much of the argument the data already does for you. Each names the statutory hook, because a petition without one is a letter.

Ask 01 · strongest

Enforce the children's safety factor that is already law

→ EPA, under FFDCA §408(b)(2)(C)
The claim
For compounds where children's measured internal dose runs roughly double adults', EPA has not demonstrated that the statutory additional tenfold safety factor for infants and children is being applied — or that the data supporting any smaller factor is reliable.
The evidence
1.83–1.88× child-to-adult body burden, creatinine-corrected, across glyphosate, TCPy and 2,4-D. NHANES, survey-weighted, n≈7,000 and 5,850.
Why it wins
The duty already exists in statute. You are not asking for a new standard, only for the agency to show its work on one it must already meet. This is the chlorpyrifos shape: specific hook, measured evidence, narrow ask.
The rebuttal
"A urinary metabolite is exposure, not risk, and levels remain far below points of departure." Answer it by asking for the aggregate-exposure demonstration, not by claiming injury.
Ask 02 · unanswerable on the merits

Require soil non-target toxicity data as a condition of registration review

→ EPA, under FIFRA §3(c)(2)(B) data call-in
The claim
EPA cannot evaluate effects on soil organisms for the majority of the mass it licenses, because the data does not exist in its own knowledgebase.
The evidence
16 of 21 major compounds have zero comparable acute soil-fauna endpoints in EPA's ECOTOX — 79% of applied mass. Glyphosate, a quarter of all US agricultural pesticide mass, has 106 soil-fauna records and none comparable.
Why it wins
It asks for information rather than prohibition, which is procedurally cheap for the agency to grant and expensive to refuse on the record. A denial has to assert the data is unnecessary.
The rebuttal
"ECOTOX is a literature compilation; registrants submit guideline studies held separately." Pre-empt it by requesting the guideline study inventory in the same petition.
Ask 03 · the soil ask, reframed

Restore conservation acreage and the capacity to deliver it

→ USDA / NRCS, via Farm Bill comment and appropriations record
The claim
Four decades of soil-loss improvement stalled when land retirement collapsed. 56.5 million acres still erode faster than soil forms.
The evidence
NRI 2022: erosion above T flat-to-rising since 2007. CRP down 70% from its 33.3M-acre plateau. Both from the agency's own release.
Why it wins
It names a lever with a budget line and an existing programme, rather than a prohibition. The FY2026 NRCS justification proposing −$776M and −2,546 staff years from Conservation Technical Assistance is both the counterargument and your exhibit.
The rebuttal
"Erosion has improved 41% — the programme worked and the land is better than it was." True, and the answer is the level, the stall, and the one-acre-in-six figure. Do not claim acceleration.
The ask to drop, or hold A blanket mandate for regenerative practice is the weakest item in the original framing — not because it is wrong, but because the federal evidence for it is model output. CEAP is simulation, not measurement. Until paired-treatment field data is in hand, a practice mandate rests on inference the agency can decline. See Step 04.
Step 04 — What would rescue the original thesis

Three acquisitions, in priority order

The soil-depletion-causes-nutrient-decline chain is currently an assertion. These are the instruments that could turn it into a finding — and one of them is closer than expected.

1. NCSS repeat-sampled pedons — 43 candidates already identified

A scan of the Kellogg Soil Survey Laboratory record found 43 locations sampled more than once, spanning 1953 to 2022. 21 have a gap of 30 years or more; the longest is 50 years. This is the only federal path to a genuine multi-decade measured soil carbon comparison.

Caveats before anyone gets excited: these are co-located candidates, not confirmed repeat pedons. Each pair needs verification that it is the same soil series and horizon, and that the carbon method is comparable across the gap — a 1960s Walkley-Black figure and a modern dry-combustion figure are not the same measurement. n=43 is small, and the sites were not chosen to be representative. But 21 verified pairs would be more measured evidence on this question than the rest of the federal record contains.

2. Same-cultivar nutrient comparison

The dilution effect is the standard counter-explanation and it is a good one. Archived-sample reanalysis — the Rothamsted Broadbalk approach — is the instrument that separates cultivar change from soil change. Without it, any nutrient-decline claim concedes the point on first contact.

3. ARS LTAR paired-treatment data

The federal home of real regenerative-versus-conventional field comparison. Smaller n, far higher internal validity than CEAP's modelling. This is what would put the practice mandate back on the table.

The design constraint that governs all of it PNSP is modelled county-level use. PDP is a rotating commodity sample. NRI is a land-point panel. NHANES is a household probability sample. ECOTOX is a literature compilation. These share no sampling frame. A causal chain built across them is descriptive association unless an explicit causal design says otherwise — and asserting causation on a join of five incompatible frames would be the entire content of the opposing comment.
