Eight analytical frameworks · one federal data mirror

The Unmeasured Acre

Eight soil-science and food-sovereignty frameworks were applied independently to the same 14.3 GB mirror of federal agricultural data. They were given different questions and no knowledge of each other's work. Seven of the eight arrived at a version of the same finding: the federal record is an instrument with a narrow shape, and most of American agriculture falls outside it.

Each panel below is a reading taken with a different instrument. The analyses apply each scholar's published analytical framework to data none of them collected; nothing here is written in anyone's voice, and no position is attributed to any living person.

Where a framework asked a question the data cannot answer, that is reported as the finding rather than replaced with a weaker chart. Three of the eight resolve that way — and they are the most consequential.

Steven Fonte Soil health, livelihoods and ecosystem services measured as one bundle — never a single number. Lavelle et al. 2014 · Fonte et al. 2012, 2023

Four of the seven outcomes have never been measured at all

Of 287 outcome-years, 56 are filled — and 41 of those are yield. Yield is observed at 6,410 times the density of soil carbon. The bundle requirement fails outright: only 4 of 21 outcome pairs can be observed in the same place in the same year, and never more than three at once.

What this is not. A count of whether joins are possible, deliberately performing none. Absence of measurement is not evidence of absence of effect.

Kate Scow Mechanistic soil biology established in long-term replicated field experiments — the antidote to “microbes fix everything”. Russell Ranch LTRAS · Tautges et al. 2019

The soil-biology evidence ladder has almost no rungs

Sorted by the strongest study design that exists anywhere in EPA's ECOTOX archive, 98.8% of the 448 million kg applied in 2018 has no record of any kind — not one laboratory test — on soil bacteria or protozoa. Glyphosate, at 26.6% of all applied mass, has zero bacterial records.

Cuts both ways. Neither “glyphosate sterilises soil” nor “glyphosate is harmless to microbes” is federally evidenced. Requiring two independent studies rather than one drops apparent field earthworm coverage from 41.4% of mass to 2.5%.

Miguel Altieri Agroecosystem redesign versus input substitution — the transition's deep step against its shallow one. Agroecology: The Science of Sustainable Agriculture

Substitution, not redesign

Counties did not stop using many pesticides — the median county still applies 89 different compounds. The mass simply collapsed onto a handful. Effective compounds per county fell from 9.0 to 4.8 while glyphosate took a third of all applied mass.

Drawn as two panels, not two axes. A dual-axis chart lets an author manufacture or erase a crossing by choosing scales. The two series share only the time axis.

Rattan Lal Soil organic carbon, erosion and degraded-soil restoration — the empirical anchor, checked in both directions. NASEM 2019 · NRCS 2022 NRI · RaCA

Forty years of erosion data, one day of carbon data

NRCS reports erosion fell 34% since 1982. True — but 89% of that decline was achieved by 1997, sheet-and-rill erosion has risen since 2007, and 56.5 million acres still erode above the tolerable rate, 3.6 million more than in 2007. The 2022 NRI runs to 222 pages and contains the word “carbon” zero times.

Single-release rule. All nine years are taken from the 2022 release. NRI back-updates its own history each cycle, so mixing releases manufactures trends that are artifacts of method revision.

Addendum Was it scrubbed, never collected, or just never reached? The three get conflated, and only one of them is reversible. NCSS Kellogg Soil Survey Laboratory · 5.4 GB, 22 tables

The second visit was never made

The United States holds measured soil carbon on 71,329 laboratory-characterised pedons, accumulated over decades. It is not scrubbed and never was — it sits behind a form postback, so no crawler following links has ever found it. We retrieved it. Then we asked how many of those locations were ever visited twice.

This is the more durable argument. Removal is real and dated — EJScreen's mapper last served a live response on 2025-01-01 and no longer resolves — but removal is contestable and reversible, and it invites a fight about whether a file moved. A missing second visit cannot be restored by republishing anything. It also converts a dead end into a working list: those 43 long-span locations are exported as ncss_repeat_sampled_sites.json, and they are the places where measured soil-carbon change over two decades can actually be computed from federal lab data.

V. Ernesto Méndez Participatory action research — farmers as co-definers of the question, not subjects of it. Méndez, Bacon & Cohen 2013 · Gliessman et al. 2023

Federal pesticide measurement is fitted to one farming system

The programme measures 51 crops; Americans grow 156. Coverage against county crop diversity is an inverted U — 5.8% at the least diverse, peaking at 92% exactly at the corn–soybean rotation, falling to 58.9% at the most diverse. The instrument is not biased against complexity in general. It is tuned to one cropping system.

Resolution contrast. The entire 2022 federal public pesticide-use survey record is 36,453 values across 21 crops. California's legally mandated reporting for the same year holds 4,281,466 individual application records.

Claire Kremen Diversified farming systems at landscape scale — outcomes governed by context beyond the farm edge. Kremen & Merenlender 2018 · Carlisle et al. 2022

The result runs against the framework — and that is the finding

More crop diversity predicts more insecticide per acre, not less (ρ = +0.413). But among the 263 counties growing no high-input crops, the relationship vanishes entirely (ρ = +0.057, p = 0.36). The national line is crop identity, not landscape structure.

The missing acquisition. The NASS Cropland Data Layer — 30 m annual crop raster, 2008–2023 — is the only source that would supply true configuration metrics. It is not in this mirror.

Johan Six Better by which metric, compared with what, on which soil, over what time horizon? Six et al. 2004 · Ellert & Bettany 1995

Same counties, same year, five different answers

The no-till/herbicide trade-off estimated five times, changing only who counts as comparable. For glyphosate: +15.3% pooled, −2.3% within the same district and crop mix. The sign flips. Crop mix alone explains R² = 0.535 of county herbicide intensity; no-till share explains 0.003.

Near-tautological by construction. USGS generates county pesticide numbers from district-level rate tables, so within-district estimates are close to a null by design and must not be read as precisely estimated zeros. And tillage was asked in 2017 and 2022 only — one overlapping year with the use data, so no county can be observed before and after a tillage change.

Leah Penniman
Soul Fire Farm Land access as prior to agronomy — who owns the land, and whose knowledge counts. Farming While Black (2018)

Counted, but not landed

Farms with at least one Black producer are 1.51% of US farms and hold 0.49% of US farmland — a land share 3.1× smaller than the farm-count share, essentially unmoved in twenty years. 150 acres against a national average of 463.

The denominator is a policy choice. Farms above $50,000 in sales are 26.9% of US farms and hold 76.9% of farmland; only 11.4% of Black-operated farms clear that bar. A per-acre instrument reaches 0.49% of that land. A per-operation instrument reaches 1.51% of those farms. Choosing between them is a distributional decision, and it is currently being made by default.

What seven of eight lenses found

They were not asked the same question. One asked who defined the measurement, one whether the system was redesigned or merely re-dosed, one what the soil biology actually establishes, one who owns the land. They converged anyway: the federal agricultural record resolves row-crop monoculture in fine detail and treats everything else as a residual. Hay, pasture, orchards, berries, vegetables and horticulture are aggregate buckets. Soil organisms have no data requirement. Landscape structure has no representation. Four of seven agroecosystem outcomes have never been measured nationally.

For a petition, this reframes the ask. An argument that residues rose or that soil carbon fell runs into a record that cannot adjudicate either. An argument that the measurement regime itself is the failure is supported directly, by the agency's own files and by 40 CFR 158.630.
