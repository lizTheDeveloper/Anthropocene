The measurement gap

Four findings from the federal pesticide record. Total applied mass in the United States has barely moved since 1992 — but what is applied has been transformed, and the systems built to watch it never followed. The strongest claim in this data is not about harm. It is about what the government has stopped being able to see.

Finding 01 — Data integrity

The decline in the published series is an artifact

Summed as distributed, USGS county-level estimates show national agricultural pesticide use falling 10.1% between 2016 and 2018. That fall is not real. Two defects produce it.

California disappears. The 2017 and 2018 files are published as noCA / NoDPR — USGS substitutes California's own full-census reporting there and omits the state. California is 12–13% of national mass. Comparing a 50-state year to a 49-state year manufactures a decline.

Rows are double-counted. From 2016 the files carry METOLACHLOR & METOLACHLOR-S and DIMETHENAMID & DIMETHENAMID-P alongside their components. The aggregate equals the component sum exactly — ratio 1.000 in all three years — so naive summation inflates 2016–18 by 8–9%.

Drop California from every year and remove the aggregate rows, and the 2016→2018 change is +0.8%. Use did not fall.

Finding 02 — Composition

Mass is flat. The chemistry underneath it is not

On the corrected basis, total applied mass rose 14.7% across twenty-six years — and just 2.3% if you hold the compound roster fixed. An argument that America is using ever more pesticide by weight does not survive this data. Say it anyway and the rebuttal is one chart.

The change is compositional. Glyphosate went from 6.9 million kg in 1992 to 119.2 million kg in 2018 — a 17-fold rise, from 1.8% of national mass to 26.6%. Everything else, summed, fell by 13.6 million kg over the same period.

One molecule replaced a large part of the American pesticide market. Methyl bromide fell 34.6 million kg, alachlor 25.7, cyanazine 15.7. The total held roughly steady because glyphosate grew into the space they left.

Finding 03 — Water monitoring

The monitoring network measures the market of 1992

If the chemistry changed, did the watching change with it? Across 18 compounds, the number of water-monitoring sites that ever tested for a compound correlates with its 1992 use — and not at all with its 2018 use.

Alachlor is the clearest case. Its use has collapsed to 0.2 million kg, a 99% fall. It is still monitored at 32,893 sites. Glyphosate, at 119.2 million kg, is monitored at 7,305 — 17% as many sites as atrazine, which it outsells nearly four to one.

The mechanism is analytical, not conspiratorial. Atrazine, metolachlor, simazine, alachlor and chlorpyrifos sit in the standard multi-residue GC-MS panels that most programs run; testing for them is nearly free once the panel runs. Glyphosate, glufosinate and paraquat each need a dedicated method. Compounds requiring one are monitored at a median 2,353 sites against 28,365 for panel compounds — a 12-fold gap, despite higher median use.

What this does not say There is no significant relationship between current use and monitoring effort — ρ = −0.14, p = 0.58, n = 18. The claim "we monitor least what we use most" is not supported and should not be made. What is supported is narrower and stronger: monitoring tracks historical use and method availability, and a dedicated-method requirement predicts sparse coverage.
Censoring Most WQP rows are non-detects carrying no numeric value — chlorpyrifos is 82% non-detect, glufosinate 98%. Dropping non-numeric rows selects on the outcome and biases any mean upward. Negative values appear in volume (simazine 17,408; alachlor 17,373) and are below-detection estimates, not concentrations. Analyse with censored methods.
Finding 04 — Soil evidence

Most of what is applied has no comparable soil-toxicity record

Residue monitoring measures what is on food. It does not attempt to measure effects on the organisms that make soil work. EPA's ECOTOX knowledgebase is the federal record of that, and against application it is close to empty where it matters most.

Of 21 major compounds, 16 have zero comparable acute soil-fauna endpoints — no LC50, EC50 or LD50 in mg/kg soil for earthworms, springtails or potworms. Those 16 account for 231.9 of 292.4 million kg applied: 79% of the mass.

Glyphosate — a quarter of all US agricultural pesticide mass — has 106 soil-fauna records in ECOTOX and zero comparable acute endpoints. The five most-applied compounds, 226 million kg a year between them, yield five comparable endpoints in total. The five least-applied yield 65.

What this does not say Absence of evidence is not evidence of safety, and it is not evidence of harm either. ECOTOX is a literature compilation: record counts track research effort, so a well-studied compound looks worse simply by being well studied. The rank correlation between use and endpoint count is not significant (ρ = −0.14, p = 0.55). The defensible claim is the coverage fact itself, which needs no correlation: most of what is applied has never been tested against soil fauna in a form anyone can compare.
Synthesis

The argument this data will carry

Three of these findings point the same way. The published use series understates what changed; the water network watches an obsolete market; the soil-toxicity record is thinnest for the compounds applied most. Each is a failure of observation, and each is demonstrable from the government's own files.

That matters because the alternative framings are weaker. Rising applied mass is not in this data. Residues above tolerance are not in this data — PDP's own headline is that over 99% of samples fall below EPA benchmarks. Soil carbon decline cannot be shown from RaCA, which is a single timepoint, or from SSURGO, which is not a time series.

What can be shown is that the American pesticide market was restructured between 1992 and 2018, and the federal instruments for observing its consequences did not follow. A petition does not have to prove harm to argue that. It has to prove that the agency cannot currently know — and that the not-knowing is concentrated precisely where the exposure is greatest.

Frames that do not join PNSP is modelled county-level use. WQP is opportunistic state and federal sampling. ECOTOX is a literature compilation. These share no sampling frame and no compound identifier — the crosswalk in this repository matches 205 of 389 PNSP compounds across sources, exactly and conservatively, leaving 84 visible for manual review. Any causal chain built across these frames is descriptive association unless an explicit causal design says otherwise.
